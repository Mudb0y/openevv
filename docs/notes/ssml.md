# SSML

**The engine reads SSML, and it reads it exactly as IBM's does.** `test/harness/ssml.sh` puts 176 documents through our reader and through IBM's own and diffs the annotations that come out: identical, character for character. That covers the whole vocabulary -- `say-as` in eleven kinds, `prosody` in four parameters and every way of writing each, `emphasis`, `voice` by gender, age, variant and name, `phoneme` in IBM's own alphabet and in IPA, `sub`, `break`, `mark`, `audio`, `p`, `s`, `metadata`, `prompt` and `xml:lang` -- along with the documents that are wrong in every way a document can be.

Nothing in the synthesiser changed for it. SSML is a preprocessor: a document becomes the annotations this engine has always understood -- `` `vs50 `` for a rate, `` `card[123] `` for a number to be read as a number, `` `[hEHlOW] `` for a pronunciation -- and is handed to the text path as though the caller had written them itself. So a caller gets say-as, prosody, IPA and voice selection at the cost of a text pass and no change to the sound.

How it was reachable at all is the part worth keeping. IBM's SSML filter was compiled into every one of the nine language object sets and nothing in those sets ever registered it: it shipped as a DLL of its own, and the two functions that would have found and loaded it -- `autoLoadFilter` and `getINIValue` -- are empty in IBM's own build. So the code was there, unreferenced, and a program linked beside those objects can hand the engine its own `ssmlFilterGetObject` through `eciRegisterFilter` and turn it on. `reference/ssmltry.c` was that program and `test/harness/ssml.c` is the harness it became. It is a sharper oracle than the audio, because what comes back is text: a wrong annotation names itself instead of showing up as a different hash over forty thousand samples.

Which languages get what is IBM's own arrangement and is worth knowing before wondering why a document did nothing. Prosody -- pitch, range and volume -- is only US and British English, French and German. Rate is those four and Japanese. Emphasis is those four and both Spanishes, Canadian French, Italian, Portuguese and Finnish, which is every language IBM shipped. Say-as is the four plus Japanese, Korean and Chinese. IPA is the four plus Japanese and Korean. A document asking for something its language has not got is ignored rather than refused, and a document in a language the build has not got produces nothing at all.

The eleventh deliberate divergence came out of this, and like the ninth and tenth it is IBM's fault rather than a choice about what to sound like. IBM's reader ends the digits of a numeric character reference by writing a NUL over the semicolon that closes them -- in the caller's own string, not in a copy -- so `&#65;` handed over as a literal page-faults on the write. `src/eci/ssml/eci_mbconvert.c` copies the digits out instead. `test/harness/ssml.c` hands both sides a writable copy of each document, which is what lets IBM's side survive the corpus at all.

**Polish reads SSML, and that part is not IBM's.** Its own language table in `ssmlmap.obj` has fifteen entries and Polish is not one of them, so `xml:lang="pl-PL"` became nothing and every element fell through; and family seventeen, which Polish is in this tree, was Thai in that table -- a language IBM shipped no name for, no voice for and no rule of, and which `LanguageId` in `src/eci/lang/eci_languageid.c` has no entry for at all, its own table stopping at fifteen. So the slot is Polish's now. That is the twelfth deliberate divergence, and what it costs is that a Thai document reads as Polish, there being no Thai in this SDK for that to matter to.

Three tables took the change: the reader's own `xml:lang` map in `src/eci/ssml/eci_ssmlmap.c`, and the two halves of the name table in `src/eci/lang/eci_languageid.c` -- family seventeen is "Pl" and "PL" going out, and "Pl" comes back in as family seventeen.

Polish is on the prosody, rate and emphasis lists as well, and deliberately not on the say-as or IPA ones. What the first three turn into is annotations, and an annotation means the same thing in every language the engine has. A say-as annotation is not: it is read by the language's own rules, and Polish's rules are still Italian's, which IBM did not list either -- so putting Polish on that list would produce a confident wrong reading of a number rather than nothing. And there is no Polish IPA converter to route a pronunciation to. So a Polish document gets rate, pitch, range, volume, emphasis, voice selection, pauses, marks, substitutions, spelling out and a pronunciation in IBM's own alphabet, and a say-as element is ignored the way it is in every other language that has not got it.

Two other things are IBM's own routing rather than the specification's, and both are kept because sounding like Eloquence is the point. `interpret-as="number"` with no format reads as an ordinal, so 123 comes out as a place rather than a count. And a Canadian French pronunciation in IPA goes through the German converter, because the language table in `win_ipatospr.obj` puts 0x30001 where 0x40000 belongs.

Two faults in the layer were found only on thirty-two bits and neither could show on sixty-four, where `__stdcall`, `__thiscall` and `__cdecl` are one convention. `src/eci/ssml/eci_ssmlstate.c` declared the environment's two entries as plain functions where they are methods, so the reader smashed its own stack the moment it asked the engine what the volume was. And the exported `ssmlFilterGetObject` was declared plain where the filter interface says stdcall, so the engine's call to it returned with the stack pointer eight bytes out. Both are the reason six builds are the gate rather than one: `probe32` failed all 90 of the new cases while `probe` passed them.

Three harnesses cover the layer and all three want Wine and IBM's objects: `test/harness/ipa.sh` over 10,934 answers, `test/harness/xmltok.sh` over 961 lines of scanner output, `test/harness/ssml.sh` over 176 documents. The recorded gate that wants neither is `test/matrix.sh`, whose `ssml` category speaks the reader's own output through the engine in all nine languages.

`docs/remaining.md` says where each of the sixteen objects went, and why the XML scanner's eight tables are lifted rather than written -- the only lifted data in `src` besides the synthesiser's own tables, and `NOTICE` says so.
