# What is left of IBM's code

Measured 26 August 2026 by counting symbols rather than by reading, and kept up to date since. Every object in `analysis/enus` was asked what it defines, and each name held against what our build defines and the mangled names the `ALIAS` lines in `src` claim. Where that comparison has since turned out to be wrong about something, the correction is written where it belongs rather than quietly folded in. Sizes are the code sections those functions sit in.

Nothing here is needed to build or to speak. `make missing` answers nothing, and has since the port finished; this is the reach of the machine and the features of the interface, not the engine. What it is for is deciding what to read next, and what never to read at all.

The 208 objects fall into four kinds. Fifty-five are a language's rules and are lifted as data rather than ported -- `ea_`, `e_`, `es_`, `et_`, `u_`, `us_`, `ut_`, `glob` and `link`, which `lang/enus` and its siblings already hold. Eight more, the `ed_` set, are empty stubs of 467 bytes. Most of the rest are ported. What follows is the remainder.

## The Delta machine's unused reach

The machine's own seventeen objects -- `access`, `assign`, `ctxt`, `ddelta`, `delta`, `dttime`, `extra`, `for`, `gener`, `heap`, `mem`, `misc`, `optimize`, `pointer`, `stack`, `test` and `optbl` -- define 444 names between them, and 139 of those looked as though they had no answer here. Those that really were absent were absent for one reason: no rule in any of the nine languages IBM shipped calls them, so the link never asked.

Three of the 139 were the count being wrong rather than a gap. `@allocDeltaHeapObject@8` and `@freeDeltaHeapObject@8` are written in `src/delta_heap.c` and have been all along; only the decoration on their names differs, since a fastcall symbol carries its argument size on x86 and carries nothing on x86-64, so holding one spelling against the other made them look absent. And `vinitloc` is ported under an adapted name, as `vinitloc_new`. That leaves 136.

This was the only part of the remainder that could block work rather than merely limit it: a Polish rule written in the upper form that wanted an operation from this list would not link, and the fix was to read that function out of IBM's object. It cannot happen any more. Every name the seventeen objects define has an answer here.

All 136 are now written, the last sixteen on 27 August 2026, and `test/prims.sh` is what proves the ones it can reach: the same table of cases compiled twice, once against our engine and once against IBM's objects, comparing what each call leaves behind. `docs/building.md` says what that harness reaches and what it does not.

Done, by object. All seven of `assign`: `settvar_l`, `settvar_lng` and `settvar_v`, with `assok`, `noass`, `chkvars` and `chkokass`, the last two being empty in the original and their argument count therefore not recoverable from it. All seven of `stack`: `npush_l`, `ncompare`, `back`, `back_nboa`, `bsclr_pushca`, `bspush_vbot` and `bspop_vbot`. All thirteen of `pointer`, which is the right register's half of the loads and the two ends -- `rpta_loadi`, `rpta_loadl`, `rpta_loadv`, `rpta_mover`, the four `tst` forms, `lpta_loadlng`, and `leftmost` and `rightmost` for both registers. Six of `mem`, which is every arithmetic operation the machine has beyond addition: `vsub`, `vmult`, `vdiv`, `vnegate`, with `divzero` and `vcompareTypeCheck`. Four of `gener`'s five, which is a generate statement's three parts and the copy that finishes one, and eleven more beside them: the same three parts again in `delta` under the names a rule uses -- `gendef_framedur`, `gendef_timestm`, `gendef_params` and their `gencur_` counterparts, which are told which cell to fill rather than reading it off the statement -- with `gen_copy`, the marks `mark_l` and `mark_lng`, and `SETCTXL` and `SETCTXR`. All five of `for`: the two walks leftwards, the two that step over the far end, and `for_cont_from`, which is `forall_cont_from` under a second name -- the same bytes in the original. Nineteen of `misc`'s twenty-one, which is the command language's own operations: sixteen of them are empty where IBM compiled them, `halt` and `abort_1` end the process, and `fail` is empty too. And three small ones from `ddelta`: `CLRNONSEQ`, `TVFLDS`, which hands back what it was given, and `streamName`. And all three of `heap` that were really missing: `peekDeltaStackStart`, `peekDeltaStackNext` and `resetDeltaStack`. And five more of the spine: `visnonseq`, which says whether anything else is threaded through a run, `vmergable`, which says which two marks may be joined, and the two calls in `delta` that stand on them, `insert_2pt` and `merge`. All thirteen of `optimize`: `if_testle_v_i`, the six `_lng` forms of the fused test-and-branch, `move_lng`, `testneq_tvars`, and the four inserts, which turned out to be one body in four widths -- a byte, two bytes as a short, two as a short of the other kind, and four as a long -- where this tree already had the first and the third. All eleven of `test`: the four bare orderings, `test_eof`, `test_hasval`, the two wide string tests, `test_time`, `test_fence`, and `test_string`, whose first byte says how wide the tokens after it are. Thirteen of `access`'s fourteen: `num_fields_in_stream`, the two `context` calls and the two `allow` ones, `init_stream`, `divide_time`, `project_sync`, the field-value walk `first_fieldval` and `next_fieldval`, `unique_value`, and `valid_prefix` with `valid_prefix_char`. Three of those thirteen the harness cannot reach and they are read rather than compared. And the last sixteen, which the section below is about: `vgen` with `generate` and `vgenerate`, the `val_expr` chain of `gcql`, `gcqr`, `val_expr`, `val_expr1`, `val_expr2`, `durcalc` and `firstdefd`, the context check of `vctxtinit`, `vclrctxt`, `vredoctxt`, `mapsyncs` and `chksyncsflags`, `merge_sync` with `safe_mergable`, `etiwinMain`, `set_saved_ptrs` and `ins_rdtoks`.

Four things that came out of reading rather than being expected. The immediate loads into the right pointer register ask the statement table what kind the *left* register's field is -- 0x44 where 0x54 was meant. It is a slip in the original and it cannot show, because both arms of the switch write the same thing; it is carried here deliberately, and the harness has a case where it could show. All four `settvar` entry points compile the same body: none of them looks at the width its name announces. And `allow_left_ctxt` and `allow_right_ctxt` are one body as well -- the right one follows the same word of the node as the left one does, which is what its own code says rather than a slip in reading it.

And `test_string`'s marker 200 is a defect that cannot be reproduced: its head takes the two-byte cell for the operand and its body decodes four bytes into the four-byte one, so what it compares is a cell nothing wrote -- in the original, whatever the stack held. Ours holds nought there, which is the one place in all of these where what is written is not what the original does, and it is a path nothing reaches.

One thing the harness turned up that is not settled. `divide_time` is a guard and a call into `vsplit_time`, which was ported long ago and which every case in the suite exercises; on the one position this harness can offer it, IBM's own `vsplit_time` faults where ours answers. No case in the suite reaches that corner, so which of the two is right is not known, and a harness that cannot get past the fault cannot find out.

`heap` is done, and how it got done is worth keeping. GNU objdump does not decode that object: the jump tables sit inside `.text` and it renders them as runs of `.byte` that swallow the instructions after them. `llvm-objdump` reads the same file cleanly, and asking it for the relocations gives the jump table's nine entries in order, which is what says which record kind costs which of the sizes the stack block carries -- the context record and the tag record the same, a token record its own length rounded up, and anything else two bytes. The harness walks the records `test_time` and `test_fence` leave and prints each kind with the step after it: 1 then 3 and 1 alternating six times, then the back marker. Changing one size shows on that line.

The last sixteen were written on 27 August 2026, having been set aside as declined first. None of them is reachable from a rule, which was the reason for declining them; the reason for writing them anyway is that a machine finished in the sense that counts symbols is a machine nobody has to open IBM's objects for again. The count is now 136 of 136.

The largest is `vgen`, 1,013 lines of disassembly and the biggest single function anywhere in the machine. It writes a generate statement out. The collected cell carries three things: the frame, whose value is how long one step lasts; the field the moment is measured in, which arrives as the cell's time; and the parameter bytes, which are a little program -- a count of statement types, and for each one its number, how many of its fields follow, and those field numbers. `vgen` walks the span the two registers mark one frame at a time and writes a line for every frame: each field asked for, worked out at that moment by `val_expr2`, with the frame's own duration put in at whichever position of the line the cell names. A first pass over the program settles where each statement type's value is to be read between, and those ends are kept in five arrays hung off the stack block, which is what `val_expr2` and `durcalc` read from; the answers are cached across frames whenever neither end has moved, which is what makes a long span affordable. `checkInterrupt` is asked between fields, so a caller who has stopped listening stops the walk. `generate` and `vgenerate` are the two names a rule would call it by, and they differ only in where the count of numbers a line holds comes from and in what a failure costs.

Three things in `vgen` are the original's and are kept rather than corrected. The buffer is not given back on the two paths that fail before the working table exists. The gap count is compared the wrong way round, so one to ten gaps give up at once, which means the branch meant to let ten through can never be taken and the test after the loop can never fire either. And the fourth of the five arrays is allocated and never checked. One good thing came out of reading it: the statement table's word at 0x38, which was `unknown_38`, is the selector `val_expr2` takes when the two ends disagree. It is `gen_sel` now, in `src/delta.h` and in `tools/delta-link.py` both, and no language in the tree names that field, so no text form moved.

The chain under it -- `gcql`, `gcqr`, `val_expr`, `val_expr1` and `val_expr2`, with `durcalc` and `firstdefd` beneath them -- is the one `src/eci_deltamisc.c` had declined years before this work, with the reason written at its foot. The head of that file says what it says instead now.

The context-consistency check is `vctxtinit` in `ctxt.obj` with `vclrctxt`, `vredoctxt`, `mapsyncs` and `chksyncsflags` in `ddelta.obj`. It is the Delta debugger's rather than the engine's: `vredoctxt` ends by printing "The delta is correct." on the command layer's own stream. The display it belongs to is still not here -- `prdelta.obj` and `rectbuf.obj`, and `src/delta_trace.c` says why -- but the check itself is written.

`merge_sync`, with the static `safe_mergable` under it, walks a node's marks either side and asks `visleft` and `visright` whether the two may be joined. `etiwinMain` is the program's entry where this engine wants the library's, and `etiwinMainDLL` beside it is the one it uses.

`set_saved_ptrs(d, was, now)` replaces one position with another everywhere the machine has written it down. Two places hold one: a word variable, whose whole value is a position, and a location a rule has pushed, whose value word is one. The pushed ones are walked a frame at a time -- from the active record up to the top, then back to the frame below it, whose extent the entry under each record gives -- so that a rule's own pushes and its callers' are all seen. What it serves is the save layer, which reads a machine back from a file where every position will land somewhere else next time; `src/delta_savefile.c` stubs that layer, so nothing here calls it.

`ins_rdtoks(d, f, l, r, arg)` walks the backtracking stack from the top, popping records and laying each one down as a token between the two positions, making a fresh mark between one token and the next. It stops at the record that marks the bottom, refuses anything that is not a token record, and with nothing pushed at all takes out whatever lies between the two instead. One detail is worth keeping: its call to `vins_sync` pushes five arguments where the callee reads four. That is the original's own doing and harmless -- the caller cleans up under cdecl -- and it is not a sign that this tree's four-argument declaration is wrong. IBM's own `vins_sync` reads 0x8 through 0x14 and never touches 0x18, which was checked.

The harness reaches three of the sixteen and compares them; the other thirteen are read. `set_saved_ptrs` is given a word variable and two pushed locations carrying the same made-up value and asked to move one of them -- made up because nothing in the call dereferences a position, it compares and replaces -- and what is printed is which of the three moved. `ins_rdtoks` is asked twice: once with the stack as the cases above it left it, where the record on top is the floor marker and it refuses, and once with a bottom marker and two pushed values built under it, where it lays a token for each, makes a mark between them, stops on the marker and answers one. That second round is what reaches `vins_tok` and `vins_sync`, and the spine printed after it is what says the tokens went where the original puts them -- where, but not what. Reading the record one byte over changes nothing printed and neither does pushing different values, both tried rather than assumed, so the content of what it lays is the same blind spot the wide inserts have. `vgen` is the third, and only half of it. A cell is built for the occasion -- the moment measured in field one, a frame fifty long, a parameter program of one statement type with one of its fields -- and what is printed is what it answers together with the two marks its first pass settled on, said as landmarks. On a machine whose spine is two nodes there is no span to walk, so the frame loop is not entered and the lines never come; the head, the allocation, the reset and the whole first pass including `gcql` and `gcqr` are. Making the first pass skip those two turns both landmarks to nothing on that line, which is how it is known to be watched. A spine with statements on it would give the rest, and that wants the whole pipeline run with an output attached.

Of the thirteen that are read, the frame loop inside `vgen` and its two callers want a language whose acoustic rules generate frames and there is none, the context check wants the display, `merge_sync` wants a rule asking to join two marks where `merge` already does it, and `etiwinMain` wants to be a program rather than a library.

So the machine is finished in both senses now. Every name in its seventeen objects has an answer in this tree, and what is left of IBM's code is elsewhere: Japanese, the SSML and XML layer, and the other engine.

## Japanese

Unchanged and written up in full in `docs/japanese.md`: about 163 KB across thirty objects, a morphological analyser rather than a table, with the 2.67 MB dictionary already lifting in one command and an oracle that builds and speaks. It is the largest genuine reverse-engineering job in the tree and the only language work outstanding.

## SSML, XML and IPA

About 76 KB across sixteen objects, and none of it runs on the path this port takes: the filter manager in `src/eci_managers.c` reports nothing present and nothing active, so annotations reach the engine directly.

`ssmlprocessor.obj` is 24 KB of tag handlers, `ssmlstate.obj` 5 KB of push, pop and validate over stacks of voice, prosody, emphasis, language and audio, `ssmlmap.obj` 7 KB mapping SSML's values onto IBM's numbers, including dates, times, currency and Roman numerals, with `ssmlsayas`, `ssmlintstack`, `ssmllangstack` and `ssmlstrstack` beside them. `filter.obj` is the base class every filter inherits, 191 bytes of it. `xmltok.obj` is 10 KB and is a lex-generated scanner, so transcribing it means reproducing generated code; writing a small parser to the same interface is the better answer if this is ever wanted. `parserxml.obj` is the 657-byte shell around it.

Two pieces separate cleanly. `win_ipatospr.obj` is 21 KB on its own, converting IPA to the SPR phoneme spelling an annotation uses, with one converter per language for US and British English, French, German, Japanese and Korean, plus UTF-8 and UCS-32 conversion. Only `ssmlprocessor` calls it, so it can be had without the rest, and it is the piece of this block most useful to a language being written. `win_languageid.obj` and `langid.obj` are the LanguageId class, 3.5 KB of bookkeeping we already do our own way.

## The public calls with no wrapper

`eci.obj` defines 130 names and we answer 68. About 14 KB of what is left is the flat C entry points: `eciDictFindFirst`, `eciDictFindNext`, `eciDictLookup` and `eciUpdateDict`, each in a plain and a wide form, with `fgetUCS2` under them; the seven filter calls, which have nothing to be a wrapper for until the filter block above exists; and `eciGeneratePhonemes`. The machinery beneath the dictionary calls is already ported -- `es_engsynDictFindFirst` in `src/eci_engsyn.c`, `eciUpdateDict` in `src/eci_api2.c` -- so those are glue rather than transcription. `win/eci_api.c` says so in its own head, and a caller asking for one of them gets nothing rather than something wrong.

## What not to transcribe

The live audio device layer. `ealaudio.obj` is 10 KB of buffer queue and callbacks, with the playback half of `soundfil.obj`, `audiocvt.obj` at 6 KB, `alaw.obj`, `mulaw.obj`, `sample.obj`, `audsize.obj` and `win_sleepcyc.obj` -- about 21 KB in all. `src/eci_pcm.c` is already the boundary where these stood, and says why: below it the original opened a Windows waveform device, and none of that survives a port. PipeWire belongs there, not in a transcription. Worth knowing that `eci.obj` and `fmtinit.obj` do reference the `eal` names, so the output-device path is where that seam shows.

The concatenative manager, 12 KB. `ConcatenationManager` loads a DLL through `LibraryLoader` and drives the Torrent engine, whose data this SDK never shipped. It belongs to the other engine; this extraction runs the formant one.

The Delta debugger. `prdelta.obj` is 11 KB drawing the spine as characters, `rectbuf.obj` 2.6 KB of the record buffer under it, with `io.obj`, and `plot.obj`, `profile.obj` and `debug.obj`, which are 8, 22 and 10 bytes. `src/delta_trace.c` already does the useful part of that job.

The skip-list translation store: `win_skipstore`, `win_skiplistnode`, `win_listnode`, `win_arraylistnode`, `win_key` and `win_translation`, with `win_crypt` and `win_cryptstdio` beside them, 11.5 KB. Nothing else in the whole object set references any of it, which was checked rather than assumed. It is IBM's compiled and encrypted dictionary format, dead in this build, and worth reading only to read or write a stock `.dct` file.

The rest is odds: `win_iniwrite` at 4 KB, wanted only by `engreg` for writing settings back; `win_vcinfo` at 4.6 KB, which asks whether ScanSoft voices are installed; `win_hz2st`, `win_stack`, the endian readers in `util.obj`, `checkExpiration` in `expire.obj` and the copyright string in `version.obj`, which nothing calls.

## The other axis

None of the above is what makes the tree IBM's. That is data, and it is already transcribed: `lang/enus` and its seven siblings, and `src/klatt_tables.c`, which `tools/gen-tables.py` writes out of `clsyn.obj` and which is what the engine sounds like. `NOTICE` says whose is whose. Replacing that is what `lang/plpl` and `make census` are for, and the day it is finished the voice stops being Eloquence, which is the point of it rather than a cost.
