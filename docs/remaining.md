# What is left of IBM's code

Measured 26 August 2026 by counting symbols rather than by reading, and kept up to date since. Every object in `analysis/enus` was asked what it defines, and each name held against what our build defines and the mangled names the `ALIAS` lines in `src` claim. Where that comparison has since turned out to be wrong about something, the correction is written where it belongs rather than quietly folded in. Sizes are the code sections those functions sit in.

Nothing here is needed to build or to speak. `make missing` answers nothing, and has since the port finished; this is the reach of the machine and the features of the interface, not the engine. What it is for is deciding what to read next, and what never to read at all.

**`make objects` is the count now, and it is a tool rather than an afternoon.** `tools/engine/census.py` asks every object in `analysis/<tag>` what it defines and looks for each name here three ways. By alias, which is exact: an `ALIAS` or `MANGLED` line claims IBM's own spelling for a function of ours, so there is nothing to argue about. By name, which is a judgement: our sources prefix a function with the class or object it came out of, so `fm_getINIValue` answers `FilterManager::getINIValue` once the prefix is off, and most of the tree is answered that way because a file-static never had an alias. And not at all, which is the answer it exists to produce.

Two rules keep the by-name half honest, and both were put in after the first run flattered itself. A bare name only answers when it is IBM's alone: eleven classes in the object set have a method called `run`, so one `th_run` of ours would otherwise have answered for all eleven. Where a name is shared the prefix has to agree too, and which prefix means which class is learned from the `ALIAS` pairs the tree already carries rather than written down anywhere. A plain C name belongs to no class and is exempt from both.

As of 4 September 2026 it answers: **1,201 names by alias, 804 by name, and 388 with no answer at all, in 20,838 instructions across 52 objects.** The 388 is the number to use. A count that matched by name only said 529, and the difference was almost all file-statics -- every one of `ecilink`'s eleven is sitting in `src/eci/bridge/eci_link.c`, and `set_dict_new`, which looked like a thousand instructions of missing work, is per-language and lives in `lang/enus/delta_sets_enus.c`.

It reads `lib` as well as `src`, `lang` and `rom`, and that was a real gap rather than tidiness: the published names are wrappers in `lib/eci_api.c`, and two of them exist nowhere else -- the filter queries, which are empty in IBM's object and are answered by being empty here. Leaving `lib` out reported those two as missing and put `eci` at twelve unanswered where it is one.

Three things it gets right for the wrong reason, and they are worth naming because the tool's own head says a by-name match is a judgement. IBM splits four of the calls into a published wrapper and an inner worker -- `eciDictLookup` beside `_eciDictLookup`, and the same for the update and the two walks -- and it also splits `eciSetParam` that way. Ours are written, but as `ed_lookup` beside `ed_dictLookup` and as one `ev_setParam` doing both jobs, so what the bare-name rule matches is IBM's inner form against our published one. The answer it gives is true and the reason it gives is not. Two of the walks are worse still for a tool: `ed_findEntry` and `ed_findEntryExt` are each one body serving two of IBM's names, parameterised by which layer call to make, so there is no alias to write that would not lie about the signature.

Two false positives are left and neither is worth contorting the tool for. `vinitloc` is ported as `vinitloc_new`, a suffix no prefix rule can reverse. And `LanguageId`'s destructor is empty in the original, which was read rather than guessed at.

Where the 388 are, in four blocks and a remainder. About 5,900 instructions are the Delta debugger's display -- `prdelta` at 55 of 56 names, `rectbuf` at 17 of 17, the four print helpers left in `deltio`, the six left in `io`, and `win_debug`, `debug`, `plot` and `profile`. About 4,900 are the live audio device -- `ealaudio`, `audiocvt`, `win_sleepcyc`, `soundfil`, `sndthrd`, `sndoutpt`, the three playback calls in `runklatt`, and `alaw`, `mulaw`, `sample` and `audsize`. 2,234 are the concatenative manager, which is no longer counted as something not to transcribe -- see below. About 3,400 are the encrypted dictionary format -- `win_cryptstdio`, `win_skipstore`, `win_translation`, `win_crypt`, `win_key` and the three list nodes. Three of those four blocks are ones the last section here says not to transcribe, and those reasons stand. The fourth, the concatenative manager, was on that list because of a claim about the SDK that was not true.

That leaves about 2,400 instructions of remainder, and it is worth listing because this is the part nobody had ever enumerated. `eci` is down to one name, `filterCheckDependencies` at 49 instructions, which finds a filter by its description and which `CheckSSMLFilterActive` in `src/eci/ssml/eci_filters.c` does the job of. `win_iniwrite` at 13 of 13 writes settings back and only `engreg`, itself 4 of 8, wants it. `win_elist_notemp` at 32 of 52 is the rest of the list template. `logio`'s six are the logical file table's teardown -- `removeInputFile`, `removeOutputFile`, `removeOutputElement`, the two `removeAll` forms and `closeOutputFile` -- and they are the only ones in this remainder that anything might reach, since nothing here ever closes a logical file. `dutil`'s seven are integer unit conversions beside the tables `src/delta/delta_tables.c` already holds. `win_filename`'s nine and `util`'s nine endian readers are called by nothing. `win_etistr` is `atoiOK` and `strncpyWithEllipsis`; `win_hz2st` and `win_stack` are two and six; `error_ds`, `win_error` and `dterror` are four error reporters between them; `rommanager`'s two are `initialRomanizer` and `getLibraryName`, private to a manager this build stubs; `win_etievent` is `ETIEvent::pulse`, `win_etithrd` is `ETIThread::getThreadID`, and `init` is a DLL entry point and `notspecialvar`. `expire`, `win_searchlib`, `win_eciloadlib`, `libmain` and `mem` are one to three names each.

**And `make stubs` is the third way a name can be absent.** `make missing` asks what our code calls that nothing of ours defines, which a linker can answer. `make objects` asks the reverse. Neither can see a function that exists, is called, and does nothing: the symbol is there, so the link is content and the census counts it as answered. That is exactly where `print_lit`, `print_var` and `print_stream` sat -- declared, empty, called by the rules seventy-eight times, with a comment above them saying printing belonged to the Delta debugger, which was true of the trace and false of the whole.

`tools/engine/stubs.py` finds them by shape rather than by comment -- a body that is only argument casts and at most a return of a constant -- and then asks IBM's object what it holds under the same name. Seven instructions is where doing nothing stops, because a thiscall method that only returns nought is seven and a plain C one is four to six; that was read off the objects rather than guessed.

Three piles come out, and as of 5 September 2026 they are 9, 87 and 21. **The nine are the ones to read**, ours doing nothing where IBM's has a body: `CVoicesInfo::GetScansoftVoices` at 279 instructions and `IsSsftVoicesAvailables` at 26, which ask Windows whether ScanSoft voices are installed and there are none; `vfstat` at 182, `vf_printf` at 106 and `vfstatall` at 38, which are the printing half that really is the debugger's -- `vf_printf`'s only caller here prints "The delta is correct."; `var_rderr` at 84 and `readErrorReport` at 37, the error-report reader, where both ends answer "give up" because no console is ever created; `deleteSleepCycle` at 49, the audio device's timing; and `AudioConverter::storeHistory` at 19. Every one of them falls inside a block this file already says not to transcribe -- which is exactly what was true of the printing primitives the day before they turned out to matter.

All nine were read against the objects on 5 September 2026 rather than left as a list. `deleteSleepCycle` is `timeEndPeriod`, `timeKillEvent`, `KillTimer` and a `free` over a Windows multimedia timer table, which is the layer `src/eci/util/eci_timer.c` replaces. `storeHistory` promotes an eight-sample filter history from a staging slot at `this+0x28` to `this+0x18` once a buffer has gone out, which is the carry-over of IBM's own converter; ours is a windowed sinc that keeps its tail inside `convertSamples`, and that is a divergence `docs/quirks.md` already records rather than a piece nobody has written.

It was eleven the day before, and the two that left were the tool's fault rather than the engine's. `translateMessage` is six instructions and `getSamples` five, both of which do nothing at all -- but each is defined in two objects, MSVC having emitted the body into every object that uses it, and the sizes were being added up. Twelve and ten, both over the seven this draws the line at. `census.ibm_sizes` takes the largest copy now, not the sum, which is also where the forty-two instructions came off the total above.

The eighty-five are faithful, IBM's being empty too, and the twenty-one are ours alone: the porting layer and the shims that stand in for Windows.

What it will not do is catch the next one by itself. A `print_lit` would appear under the first pile with its comment saying why it was fine. What it does is keep that pile to nine, so a person can re-read every reason in an afternoon rather than leaving them scattered through comments nobody revisits.

The 208 objects fall into four kinds. Fifty-five are a language's rules and are lifted as data rather than ported -- `ea_`, `e_`, `es_`, `et_`, `u_`, `us_`, `ut_`, `glob` and `link`, which `lang/enus` and its siblings already hold. Eight more, the `ed_` set, are empty stubs of 467 bytes. Most of the rest are ported. What follows is the remainder.

## The Delta machine's unused reach

The machine's own seventeen objects -- `access`, `assign`, `ctxt`, `ddelta`, `delta`, `dttime`, `extra`, `for`, `gener`, `heap`, `mem`, `misc`, `optimize`, `pointer`, `stack`, `test` and `optbl` -- define 444 names between them, and 139 of those looked as though they had no answer here. Those that really were absent were absent for one reason: no rule in any of the nine languages IBM shipped calls them, so the link never asked.

Three of the 139 were the count being wrong rather than a gap. `@allocDeltaHeapObject@8` and `@freeDeltaHeapObject@8` are written in `src/delta/delta_heap.c` and have been all along; only the decoration on their names differs, since a fastcall symbol carries its argument size on x86 and carries nothing on x86-64, so holding one spelling against the other made them look absent. And `vinitloc` is ported under an adapted name, as `vinitloc_new`. That leaves 136.

This was the only part of the remainder that could block work rather than merely limit it: a Polish rule written in the upper form that wanted an operation from this list would not link, and the fix was to read that function out of IBM's object. It cannot happen any more. Every name the seventeen objects define has an answer here.

All 136 are now written, the last sixteen on 27 August 2026, and `test/harness/prims.sh` is what proves the ones it can reach: the same table of cases compiled twice, once against our engine and once against IBM's objects, comparing what each call leaves behind. `docs/testing.md` says what that harness reaches and what it does not.

Done, by object. All seven of `assign`: `settvar_l`, `settvar_lng` and `settvar_v`, with `assok`, `noass`, `chkvars` and `chkokass`, the last two being empty in the original and their argument count therefore not recoverable from it. All seven of `stack`: `npush_l`, `ncompare`, `back`, `back_nboa`, `bsclr_pushca`, `bspush_vbot` and `bspop_vbot`. All thirteen of `pointer`, which is the right register's half of the loads and the two ends -- `rpta_loadi`, `rpta_loadl`, `rpta_loadv`, `rpta_mover`, the four `tst` forms, `lpta_loadlng`, and `leftmost` and `rightmost` for both registers. Six of `mem`, which is every arithmetic operation the machine has beyond addition: `vsub`, `vmult`, `vdiv`, `vnegate`, with `divzero` and `vcompareTypeCheck`. Four of `gener`'s five, which is a generate statement's three parts and the copy that finishes one, and eleven more beside them: the same three parts again in `delta` under the names a rule uses -- `gendef_framedur`, `gendef_timestm`, `gendef_params` and their `gencur_` counterparts, which are told which cell to fill rather than reading it off the statement -- with `gen_copy`, the marks `mark_l` and `mark_lng`, and `SETCTXL` and `SETCTXR`. All five of `for`: the two walks leftwards, the two that step over the far end, and `for_cont_from`, which is `forall_cont_from` under a second name -- the same bytes in the original. Nineteen of `misc`'s twenty-one, which is the command language's own operations: sixteen of them are empty where IBM compiled them, `halt` and `abort_1` end the process, and `fail` is empty too. And three small ones from `ddelta`: `CLRNONSEQ`, `TVFLDS`, which hands back what it was given, and `streamName`. And all three of `heap` that were really missing: `peekDeltaStackStart`, `peekDeltaStackNext` and `resetDeltaStack`. And five more of the spine: `visnonseq`, which says whether anything else is threaded through a run, `vmergable`, which says which two marks may be joined, and the two calls in `delta` that stand on them, `insert_2pt` and `merge`. All thirteen of `optimize`: `if_testle_v_i`, the six `_lng` forms of the fused test-and-branch, `move_lng`, `testneq_tvars`, and the four inserts, which turned out to be one body in four widths -- a byte, two bytes as a short, two as a short of the other kind, and four as a long -- where this tree already had the first and the third. All eleven of `test`: the four bare orderings, `test_eof`, `test_hasval`, the two wide string tests, `test_time`, `test_fence`, and `test_string`, whose first byte says how wide the tokens after it are. Thirteen of `access`'s fourteen: `num_fields_in_stream`, the two `context` calls and the two `allow` ones, `init_stream`, `divide_time`, `project_sync`, the field-value walk `first_fieldval` and `next_fieldval`, `unique_value`, and `valid_prefix` with `valid_prefix_char`. Three of those thirteen the harness cannot reach and they are read rather than compared. And the last sixteen, which the section below is about: `vgen` with `generate` and `vgenerate`, the `val_expr` chain of `gcql`, `gcqr`, `val_expr`, `val_expr1`, `val_expr2`, `durcalc` and `firstdefd`, the context check of `vctxtinit`, `vclrctxt`, `vredoctxt`, `mapsyncs` and `chksyncsflags`, `merge_sync` with `safe_mergable`, `etiwinMain`, `set_saved_ptrs` and `ins_rdtoks`.

Four things that came out of reading rather than being expected. The immediate loads into the right pointer register ask the statement table what kind the *left* register's field is -- 0x44 where 0x54 was meant. It is a slip in the original and it cannot show, because both arms of the switch write the same thing; it is carried here deliberately, and the harness has a case where it could show. All four `settvar` entry points compile the same body: none of them looks at the width its name announces. And `allow_left_ctxt` and `allow_right_ctxt` are one body as well -- the right one follows the same word of the node as the left one does, which is what its own code says rather than a slip in reading it.

And `test_string`'s marker 200 is a defect that cannot be reproduced: its head takes the two-byte cell for the operand and its body decodes four bytes into the four-byte one, so what it compares is a cell nothing wrote -- in the original, whatever the stack held. Ours holds nought there, which is the one place in all of these where what is written is not what the original does, and it is a path nothing reaches.

One thing the harness turned up that is not settled. `divide_time` is a guard and a call into `vsplit_time`, which was ported long ago and which every case in the suite exercises; on the one position this harness can offer it, IBM's own `vsplit_time` faults where ours answers. No case in the suite reaches that corner, so which of the two is right is not known, and a harness that cannot get past the fault cannot find out.

`heap` is done, and how it got done is worth keeping. GNU objdump does not decode that object: the jump tables sit inside `.text` and it renders them as runs of `.byte` that swallow the instructions after them. `llvm-objdump` reads the same file cleanly, and asking it for the relocations gives the jump table's nine entries in order, which is what says which record kind costs which of the sizes the stack block carries -- the context record and the tag record the same, a token record its own length rounded up, and anything else two bytes. The harness walks the records `test_time` and `test_fence` leave and prints each kind with the step after it: 1 then 3 and 1 alternating six times, then the back marker. Changing one size shows on that line.

The last sixteen were written on 27 August 2026, having been set aside as declined first. None of them is reachable from a rule, which was the reason for declining them; the reason for writing them anyway is that a machine finished in the sense that counts symbols is a machine nobody has to open IBM's objects for again. The count is now 136 of 136.

The largest is `vgen`, 1,013 lines of disassembly and the biggest single function anywhere in the machine. It writes a generate statement out. The collected cell carries three things: the frame, whose value is how long one step lasts; the field the moment is measured in, which arrives as the cell's time; and the parameter bytes, which are a little program -- a count of statement types, and for each one its number, how many of its fields follow, and those field numbers. `vgen` walks the span the two registers mark one frame at a time and writes a line for every frame: each field asked for, worked out at that moment by `val_expr2`, with the frame's own duration put in at whichever position of the line the cell names. A first pass over the program settles where each statement type's value is to be read between, and those ends are kept in five arrays hung off the stack block, which is what `val_expr2` and `durcalc` read from; the answers are cached across frames whenever neither end has moved, which is what makes a long span affordable. `checkInterrupt` is asked between fields, so a caller who has stopped listening stops the walk. `generate` and `vgenerate` are the two names a rule would call it by, and they differ only in where the count of numbers a line holds comes from and in what a failure costs.

Three things in `vgen` are the original's and are kept rather than corrected. The buffer is not given back on the two paths that fail before the working table exists. The gap count is compared the wrong way round, so one to ten gaps give up at once, which means the branch meant to let ten through can never be taken and the test after the loop can never fire either. And the fourth of the five arrays is allocated and never checked. One good thing came out of reading it: the statement table's word at 0x38, which was `unknown_38`, is the selector `val_expr2` takes when the two ends disagree. It is `gen_sel` now, in `src/delta/delta.h` and in `tools/module/link.py` both, and no language in the tree names that field, so no text form moved.

The chain under it -- `gcql`, `gcqr`, `val_expr`, `val_expr1` and `val_expr2`, with `durcalc` and `firstdefd` beneath them -- is the one `src/eci/bridge/eci_deltamisc.c` had declined years before this work, with the reason written at its foot. The head of that file says what it says instead now.

The context-consistency check is `vctxtinit` in `ctxt.obj` with `vclrctxt`, `vredoctxt`, `mapsyncs` and `chksyncsflags` in `ddelta.obj`. It is the Delta debugger's rather than the engine's: `vredoctxt` ends by printing "The delta is correct." on the command layer's own stream. The display it belongs to is still not here -- `prdelta.obj` and `rectbuf.obj`, and `src/delta/delta_trace.c` says why -- but the check itself is written.

`merge_sync`, with the static `safe_mergable` under it, walks a node's marks either side and asks `visleft` and `visright` whether the two may be joined. `etiwinMain` is the program's entry where this engine wants the library's, and `etiwinMainDLL` beside it is the one it uses.

`set_saved_ptrs(d, was, now)` replaces one position with another everywhere the machine has written it down. Two places hold one: a word variable, whose whole value is a position, and a location a rule has pushed, whose value word is one. The pushed ones are walked a frame at a time -- from the active record up to the top, then back to the frame below it, whose extent the entry under each record gives -- so that a rule's own pushes and its callers' are all seen. What it serves is the save layer, which reads a machine back from a file where every position will land somewhere else next time; `src/delta/delta_savefile.c` stubs that layer, so nothing here calls it.

`ins_rdtoks(d, f, l, r, arg)` walks the backtracking stack from the top, popping records and laying each one down as a token between the two positions, making a fresh mark between one token and the next. It stops at the record that marks the bottom, refuses anything that is not a token record, and with nothing pushed at all takes out whatever lies between the two instead. One detail is worth keeping: its call to `vins_sync` pushes five arguments where the callee reads four. That is the original's own doing and harmless -- the caller cleans up under cdecl -- and it is not a sign that this tree's four-argument declaration is wrong. IBM's own `vins_sync` reads 0x8 through 0x14 and never touches 0x18, which was checked.

The harness reaches three of the sixteen and compares them; the other thirteen are read. `set_saved_ptrs` is given a word variable and two pushed locations carrying the same made-up value and asked to move one of them -- made up because nothing in the call dereferences a position, it compares and replaces -- and what is printed is which of the three moved. `ins_rdtoks` is asked twice: once with the stack as the cases above it left it, where the record on top is the floor marker and it refuses, and once with a bottom marker and two pushed values built under it, where it lays a token for each, makes a mark between them, stops on the marker and answers one. That second round is what reaches `vins_tok` and `vins_sync`, and the spine printed after it is what says the tokens went where the original puts them -- where, but not what. Reading the record one byte over changes nothing printed and neither does pushing different values, both tried rather than assumed, so the content of what it lays is the same blind spot the wide inserts have. `vgen` is the third, and only half of it. A cell is built for the occasion -- the moment measured in field one, a frame fifty long, a parameter program of one statement type with one of its fields -- and what is printed is what it answers together with the two marks its first pass settled on, said as landmarks. On a machine whose spine is two nodes there is no span to walk, so the frame loop is not entered and the lines never come; the head, the allocation, the reset and the whole first pass including `gcql` and `gcqr` are. Making the first pass skip those two turns both landmarks to nothing on that line, which is how it is known to be watched. A spine with statements on it would give the rest, and that wants the whole pipeline run with an output attached.

Of the thirteen that are read, the frame loop inside `vgen` and its two callers want a language whose acoustic rules generate frames and there is none, the context check wants the display, `merge_sync` wants a rule asking to join two marks where `merge` already does it, and `etiwinMain` wants to be a program rather than a library.

So the machine is finished in both senses now. Every name in its seventeen objects has an answer in this tree, and what is left of IBM's code is elsewhere: Japanese and the other engine.

## Japanese

Written up in full in `docs/japanese.md`: a morphological analyser rather than a table, with the 2.67 MB dictionary already lifting in one command and an oracle that builds and speaks. It is the largest genuine reverse-engineering job in the tree and the only language work outstanding. Twenty-one of the thirty-five romanizer objects are written whole, the reader and the dictionary search and the path search and the phrase buffer and the number reader and the intonation and the prosody chain among them, so what is left is 113 entry points in six classes: the spine, what makes the readable form, and `TextAnalysis` and `PhraseTable` together as the largest single piece.

## SSML, XML and IPA

Done, on 3 September 2026. All sixteen objects, about 76 KB, and the layer runs: `test/harness/ssml.sh` puts 176 documents through our reader and through IBM's and gets the same annotations out of both, character for character.

What made it possible is that IBM's SSML filter was compiled into every one of the nine language object sets and nothing in them ever registered it. It shipped as a DLL of its own, and the two things that would have found it -- `autoLoadFilter` and `getINIValue` -- are empty in IBM's own build. So the object was there, unreferenced, and a program linked beside those objects can hand the engine its own `ssmlFilterGetObject` through `eciRegisterFilter` and ask what it made of a string. That is a sharper oracle than the audio: what comes back is text, so a wrong annotation names itself instead of showing up as a different hash over forty thousand samples.

Where each object went. `ssmlprocessor.obj`, 24 KB of tag handlers, is `src/eci/ssml/eci_ssmlprocessor.c`. `ssmlstate.obj` is `src/eci/ssml/eci_ssmlstate.c` with the five say-as counters beside it in `src/eci/ssml/eci_ssmlsayas.c`, and the three stacks are `src/eci/ssml/eci_ssmlintstack.c`, `src/eci/ssml/eci_ssmlstrstack.c` and `src/eci/ssml/eci_ssmllangstack.c`. `ssmlmap.obj` is `src/eci/ssml/eci_ssmlmap.c`, which is where a rate in words a minute becomes the engine's number and where dates, times, currency, booleans and Roman numerals are read. `ssmlutils.obj` is `src/eci/ssml/eci_ssmlutils.c`. `filter.obj` and `ssmlfilter.obj` are `src/eci/ssml/eci_filter.c` and `src/eci/ssml/eci_ssmlfilter.c`; `filtermanager.obj` is `src/eci/ssml/eci_filtermanager.c`, which replaced the stubs `src/eci/synth/eci_managers.c` used to hold. `win_mbconvert.obj` is `src/eci/ssml/eci_mbconvert.c`, `win_ipatospr.obj` is `src/eci/ssml/eci_ipatospr.c`, and `win_languageid.obj` and `langid.obj` are `src/eci/lang/eci_languageid.c` and `src/eci/lang/eci_langid.c`.

`xmltok.obj` was the one that looked like a wall. It is flex 2.5.4 output with REJECT in it, so transcribing it means reproducing generated code -- and the note that used to stand here said writing a small parser to the same interface was the better answer. That was wrong, and the eight tables are why. A rule set that has been compiled into a state machine cannot be re-derived from the machine: the equivalence classes, the base and default rows and the accepting lists are the only surviving statement of what the forty-eight rules were. `tools/engine/xmltok.py` slices those eight tables out of the object and writes `src/eci/ssml/eci_xmltok_tables.c`, and `--check` holds the tree against the object; the skeleton around them is written out by hand in `src/eci/ssml/eci_xmltok.c`, with IBM's forty-eight actions in it. `parserxml.obj`, the shell, is `src/eci/ssml/eci_parserxml.c`. `test/harness/xmltok.sh` holds the scanner alone against IBM's over 46 documents in three modes.

The block is closed by name as well as by behaviour, which was checked rather than assumed: every function every one of the seventeen objects defines has a counterpart here. Three kinds of name have none and each is accounted for. The compiler's scalar and vector destructor helpers -- `??_G`, `??_E`, `??_H`, `??_I` -- belong to vtables ours does not have. The flex skeleton's own statics -- `yyunput`, `yy_get_next_buffer`, `yy_try_NUL_trans` and the rest -- are static here too and so carry no alias. And `LanguageId`'s destructor is empty in the original, which was read rather than guessed at.

Three harnesses came out of this and all three say identical: `test/harness/ipa.sh` over 10,934 answers, `test/harness/xmltok.sh` over 961 lines, `test/harness/ssml.sh` over 176 documents. All three want Wine and IBM's objects. The recorded gate that does not is `test/matrix.sh`, whose `ssml` category speaks the reader's output through the engine in all nine languages.

Two things are IBM's own routing rather than the specification's and are kept. `interpret-as="number"` with no format reads as an ordinal, so 123 comes out as a place rather than a count. And a Canadian French IPA pronunciation goes through the German converter, because the language table in `win_ipatospr.obj` puts 0x30001 where 0x40000 belongs.

Two things are deliberate divergences and both are in the list in `docs/status.md`. IBM's reader ends the digits of a numeric character reference by writing a NUL over the semicolon that ends them -- in the caller's own string, not in a copy -- so `&#65;` in a literal page-faults. Ours copies the digits out instead.

And Polish is in the reader's language table, where IBM's has Thai. Family seventeen is Polish in this tree, and it was Thai in `ssmlmap.obj` and nothing at all in `win_languageid.obj`, whose own table stops at fifteen -- so a Polish document became a number nothing could name and every element fell through. Polish is on the prosody, rate and emphasis lists as well, and deliberately not on the say-as or IPA ones, because a say-as annotation is read by the language's own rules and Polish's are still Italian's.

## The published interface, closed

`eci.obj` publishes seventy-one `eci*` entry points and the library exports all seventy-one, with nothing exported that IBM does not publish. That was settled by diffing the two export tables rather than by counting, and it is what `make objects` will say of that object from here.

The last ten went in on 4 September 2026. Eight are the dictionary calls -- `eciDictLookup`, `eciDictFindFirst`, `eciDictFindNext` and `eciUpdateDict`, each in a plain form and in an extended form carrying a part of speech -- and they are in `src/eci/dict/eci_dict.c` beside the rest of the dictionary glue. Two are `eciGetAvailableFilters` and `eciGetFilterDescription`, and both are empty in IBM's own object: they answer nought and never touch what the caller handed them, which was read rather than assumed. `fgetUCS2` under them is written too, with the one divergence its head names -- the original asks the stream's own flag word what Microsoft's runtime put there and this asks `feof` and `ferror`, which are the same two questions.

Three things came out of writing the eight and are worth keeping.

**A set has four volumes, not three.** `eciMainDictExt` keeps a part of speech beside each entry and exists only for a language written in another script -- Chinese in four code sets, Korean in two, Japanese in two. Every other language answers `eciDictInvalidVolume`, and that answer is most of what those calls do for anything in this tree. All eight carry the same switch on the language to decide, which was checked by comparing the constants their compares hold rather than assumed; `ed_road` is the one copy of it here. One asymmetry in it is IBM's own slip and is kept: Chinese code set two reaches the extended road in its second dialect and not its first.

**Two instance fields are named now.** What were `direct` and `direct2` with twelve unknown bytes after them are the five slots the dictionary layer answers into: the translation, the key, a length for each and the part of speech. And `owned1` and `owned2` are the narrow copies handed back to the caller, which the instance frees on the next call. Nothing but these eight calls reads any of them, which is why the names were not knowable before.

**`eciDictLookupA` answers an error where the plain form answers the string**, and it ends by turning an empty answer into `eciDictNoEntry` -- a test it makes even on the roads that never write the caller's pointer, so an uncleared variable can turn an invalid volume into no entry. The first version written here answered the string, and `make dict` is what said so: the same driver against IBM's objects and against ours, thirty-two answers, every one now identical.

The eight filter calls that used to be listed here are written, in `src/eci/ssml/eci_filters.c`: `eciRegisterFilter`, `eciUnregisterFilter`, `eciNewFilter`, `eciDeleteFilter`, `eciActivateFilter`, `eciDeactivateFilter`, `eciSetFilter` and `eciUpdateFilter`. Two of them answer oddly and faithfully. `eciActivateFilter` reports a refusal over a filter it has just turned on, because the manager's answer is always the refusal whatever it did. And `eciUpdateFilter` works out an answer, stores it, and returns nought.

`eciGeneratePhonemes` is written as well, and what it cost was not the entry point but the Delta runtime's printing layer under it, which `docs/status.md` describes.

## What not to transcribe

The live audio device layer. `ealaudio.obj` is 10 KB of buffer queue and callbacks, with the playback half of `soundfil.obj`, `audiocvt.obj` at 6 KB, `alaw.obj`, `mulaw.obj`, `sample.obj`, `audsize.obj` and `win_sleepcyc.obj` -- about 21 KB in all. `src/eci/sound/eci_pcm.c` is already the boundary where these stood, and says why: below it the original opened a Windows waveform device, and none of that survives a port. PipeWire belongs there, not in a transcription. Worth knowing that `eci.obj` and `fmtinit.obj` do reference the `eal` names, so the output-device path is where that seam shows.

The concatenative engine, and it stays off the list for a better reason than the one that used to be given. The old sentence said the Torrent engine's data was never shipped. That was false: it is shipped, and so is the engine. The real reason is that it is not Eloquence.

`DATA/eCTTS/Voices` in the SDK holds six of them -- `de_de`, `en_gb`, `en_us`, `fr_ca`, `it_it`, `ja_jp` -- one `le/E2MLAN00.LE` apiece, 6.8 to 11 MB each and 55 MB in all. `ecictts.dll` is beside them at 0.4 MB. And the engine is in the static libraries as well: the six `...C.lib` files, one for each of those six languages, carry 74 objects the plain libraries do not, with symbols, in exactly the form everything else here was lifted from. 82,119 instructions, against 505,338 for the whole of IBM's English object set: about a sixth of everything, and four times the 20,838 this file counts as left.

What is in there is a unit-selection pipeline and its waveform reconstruction. `sent2words`, `words2phones`, `phones2leaves`, `leaves2occs_haifa`, `occs2speech_haifa` is the spine of it; `synth_haifa` at 3,516 instructions and `torrentinst` at 4,525 are the largest single pieces; `librvcReconstruction` is twelve objects of it -- `rvc_ifft`, `rvc_rec_minph`, `rvc_rec_phase`, `rvc_env` and their tables -- and `libeTTS` seven more. `concatmanager.obj` itself is 3,514 instructions in that build rather than the 2,234 the formant one carries, so even the manager is bigger than it looks from here.

Twenty-three of the 74 are the ECI layer recompiled as `STATIC_SYNTH_CONCAT` -- `eci`, `synthrd`, `sndthrd`, `msgqueue` and their neighbours under names already ported here. Those are differences to fold into files that exist rather than new files.

So it is liftable, and that is worth having written down. It is still not going to be lifted here, because this project is Eloquence and Eloquence is the formant engine. That is Eloquent Technology's synthesiser, which IBM bought; the concatenative one is IBM's own later work, and the objects say so themselves -- `synth_haifa`, `occs2speech_haifa`, `ttsnode_haifa`, a dozen more, named for the lab that wrote them. ETI never shipped anything like it. Someone who wants that engine has everything they need to lift it and this file has sized the job, but it is a different product wearing the same interface, and reproducing it would not make anything here sound more like Eloquence.

The Delta debugger. `prdelta.obj` is 11 KB drawing the spine as characters, `rectbuf.obj` 2.6 KB of the record buffer under it, with `io.obj`, and `plot.obj`, `profile.obj` and `debug.obj`, which are 8, 22 and 10 bytes. `src/delta/delta_trace.c` already does the useful part of that job.

The skip-list translation store: `win_skipstore`, `win_skiplistnode`, `win_listnode`, `win_arraylistnode`, `win_key` and `win_translation`, with `win_crypt` and `win_cryptstdio` beside them, 11.5 KB. Nothing else in the whole object set references any of it, which was checked rather than assumed. It is IBM's compiled and encrypted dictionary format, dead in this build, and worth reading only to read or write a stock `.dct` file.

The rest is odds: `win_iniwrite` at 4 KB, wanted only by `engreg` for writing settings back; `win_vcinfo` at 4.6 KB, which asks whether ScanSoft voices are installed; `win_hz2st`, `win_stack`, the endian readers in `util.obj`, `checkExpiration` in `expire.obj` and the copyright string in `version.obj`, which nothing calls.

## The other axis

None of the above is what makes the tree IBM's. That is data, and it is already transcribed: `lang/enus` and its seven siblings, and `src/klatt/klatt_tables.c`, which `tools/engine/klatt-tables.py` writes out of `clsyn.obj` and which is what the engine sounds like. `NOTICE` says whose is whose. Replacing that is what `lang/plpl` and `make census` are for, and the day it is finished the voice stops being Eloquence, which is the point of it rather than a cost.
